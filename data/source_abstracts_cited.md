# Source Abstracts — ESG Data Source Matrix (Artefact 2), with citations

Citations are placed at the claim they support, marked in **bold**. Where an abstract draws on more than one source, each claim carries its own citation. Two sources (Bundeslaender Open Data, WLV Gefahrenzonenplan) are documented in the matrix only via the generic portal **data.gv.at** rather than a specific landing page; this is flagged rather than a more specific URL invented. ## Physical risk sources

### Climate-ADAPT (eea_climate_adapt)
Climate-ADAPT is a public knowledge portal operated by the **European Environment Agency (EEA)** **(EEA, n.d.-a)**. It hosts the European Climate Data Explorer (ECDE), which provides 32 pre-computed, peer-reviewed climate hazard indices covering 16 hazard categories, grouped into six IPCC AR6 climatic-impact-driver types (heat and cold, wet and dry, wind, snow and ice, coastal, open ocean); the indicator dataset itself is published on the Copernicus Climate Data Store as a distinct, downloadable/queryable dataset **(EEA/C3S, n.d.)**. Not all of the matrix's physical hazards are represented: permafrost, landslide and soil erosion have no corresponding index in this set, while heat stress and water stress are only indirectly covered via proxy indices (Universal Thermal Climate Index; aridity) **(EEA, 2021)**. Grid resolution varies by indicator, from 5km (hydrology-based indices such as river flood and aridity) to roughly 0.1-0.25 degrees (~11-28km) for most temperature, precipitation, wind and fire-weather indices **(EEA/C3S, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through an interactive web portal, a queryable API and direct download of the underlying indicator dataset, and is free to use. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it provides ready-made, peer-reviewed hazard indices with documented methodology, saving the index-definition effort that raw Copernicus CDS variables would require, although three hazards are not covered at all and its grid resolution remains coarser than the parcel-level Austrian sources for acute hazards. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is only partly ready: once geocoded, the indicator dataset can be queried per coordinate, but resolution varies by indicator (5km to ~28km), so multiple exposures within the same grid cell share a value.

### Risk Data Hub (jrc_risk_data_hub)
The Risk Data Hub (RDH) is a public multi-hazard risk and loss data platform operated by the **Joint Research Centre (JRC)** in cooperation with the EU Disaster Risk Management Knowledge Centre (DRMKC) **(JRC DRMKC, n.d.)**. It provides modelled exposure, hazard and historical-event data covering multiple physical hazards at regional (NUTS 2/3) granularity across EU, EFTA and IPA countries **(JRC DRMKC, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through a web map portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source and secondary to the Geography dimension (0090–0130). For an SNCI, the source is suitable because it combines primary relevance with EU-wide consistent methodology, although its Vulnerability and Risk Analysis modules remain documented by JRC as ongoing work, and finer layers require geocoding and spatial overlay. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is only partly ready: once geocoded and joined to its NUTS2/3 modules, it differentiates between regions but not between individual exposures within the same region.

### European Forest Fire Information System (effis_wildfire)
EFFIS is a public wildfire information service operated jointly by the **JRC and Copernicus Emergency Management Service** **(JRC/Copernicus EMS, n.d.)**. It provides daily fire danger indices and historical burnt-area data covering wildfire risk at grid/raster granularity across Europe and the MENA region **(JRC/Copernicus EMS, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through an OGC map service and direct download, and is free of charge under a CC-BY 4.0 licence requiring attribution only. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it is the only dedicated wildfire dataset identified, although the raw hazard-index and event data require own geocoding and GIS overlay before they can support exposure-level classification. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is only partly ready: once geocoded and queried against its ~1km fire-danger raster, it differentiates across the country but not within a single town or parcel.

### Copernicus Climate Data Store (copernicus_cds)
The CDS is a public climate data repository operated by **ECMWF on behalf of the EU** **(ECMWF, n.d.)**, comprising over 140 datasets of raw and derived climate variables and reanalysis/projection data. This entry covers the general-purpose raw variables (e.g. ERA5 temperature, precipitation, wind fields); the standalone, ready-made hazard indicator products hosted on the same CDS (ERA5-HEAT for heat stress, the SMHI hydrology dataset for flood/water stress/soil-moisture drought, CEMS Fire Danger for wildfire) are documented separately under copernicus_climate_indicators, since they differ materially in processing effort and portfolio-readiness. The source is accessed through a web portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge, uniformly licensed under CC-BY 4.0 since July 2025 (individual sub-datasets may carry stricter terms). In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source and secondary to the Geography dimension. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it is authoritative and EU-wide consistent, although the raw NetCDF/GRIB climate variables require climatological and GIS processing before they translate into an exposure classification. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is not ready for typical Austrian portfolios: its roughly 0.1-0.25 degree grid means most exposures within a regionally concentrated portfolio would share an identical value.

### Copernicus Ready-Made Climate Indicators (copernicus_climate_indicators)
This is a set of standalone, ready-made physical hazard indicator products, published as individual datasets on the **Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS)**, distinct from the general-purpose raw climate variables covered by the separate copernicus_cds entry **(ECMWF, n.d.)**. It comprises: ERA5-HEAT (UTCI/heat stress, peer-reviewed, ECMWF), the SMHI hydrology dataset (flood, water stress/aridity, soil-moisture drought, produced by the Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute for C3S), and CEMS Fire Danger (wildfire, produced by JRC/Copernicus Emergency Management Service) **(Di Napoli et al., 2021; SMHI/C3S, n.d.)**. Each is directly downloadable and queryable via the CDS API, and is free to use under CC-BY 4.0-equivalent terms. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because each product is a purpose-built, documented hazard indicator with no own index-definition effort required, unlike raw Copernicus CDS variables, although its hazard breadth is narrower than the full Copernicus CDS catalogue (only heat stress, flood, water stress, soil-moisture drought and wildfire are covered this way). In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is ready: once geocoded, each product returns a differentiating, grid-based value directly, with no further climatological processing required.

### EBA ESG Dashboard (eba_esg_dashboard)
The EBA ESG Dashboard is a public statistics dashboard operated by the **European Banking Authority (EBA)** **(EBA, n.d.-a)**. It provides aggregated ESG risk indicators at EU bank level, covering both physical and transition risk dimensions at institution/portfolio-level granularity rather than individual exposure level **(EBA, n.d.-a)**. The source is accessed through a web-based statistics dashboard and is free to use. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant only as a benchmarking reference, not to any specific data point. For an SNCI, the source is not suitable for populating individual exposures, although it remains useful for peer-comparison and contextual benchmarking of the institution's own ESG risk profile. This is a technical limitation rather than a granularity one: the output is aggregate bank-level statistics, with no per-exposure data to draw on at all.

### ECB Climate-Related Physical Risk Indicators (ecb_physical_risk_indicators)
This is a public methodological and statistical resource operated by the **European Central Bank (ECB)** **(ECB, n.d.)**. It provides a documented methodology and euro-area statistics on physical risk exposure, covering physical hazards at institution/portfolio-level granularity (NUTS3-based underlying methodology) **(ECB, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through a statistics dashboard and is free to use, though the underlying data (AnaCredit/RIAD) are not accessible to an SNCI. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data points 0050 and 0090–0130 as a secondary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because its documented methodology can be replicated using AnaCredit/JRC/Copernicus-equivalent inputs, although the ECB's own underlying supervisory data are unavailable to a non-significant institution. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source itself is not ready: its output is aggregate/dashboard statistics with no per-exposure data, so any per-exposure use means replicating the methodology with the bank's own data rather than drawing on the ECB source directly.

### Hochwasserüberflutungsflächen HQ30/HQ100/HQ300 (hq_flood_extent)
This is a national flood extent dataset, published via the **INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich**, with the **Bundesministerium fuer Land- und Forstwirtschaft, Klima- und Umweltschutz, Regionen und Wasserwirtschaft (BMLUK)** as data owner and **Umweltbundesamt GmbH** as the technical operator **(INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-b)**. It combines Abflussuntersuchungen (ABU), HORA data and, in part, the BWV and WLV hazard zoning plans into a single set of flood extent layers for the 30-, 100- and 300-year return periods, covering flood risk at parcel/address-level granularity across Austria **(INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-b)**. The source is accessed through OGC WMS/WFS services and direct GML/shapefile download via the INSPIRE Atom download service, and is free of charge under a CC BY 4.0 licence. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because it combines several of the other Austrian hazard sources into ready-made flood extent layers with a standard OGC interface, without HORA's bulk-access restriction. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is ready: once geocoded, a WFS query or a local spatial join against the downloaded layer assigns a differentiating, parcel-level value to each exposure without further manual adjustment.

### WLV Gefahrenzonenplan (wlv_gefahrenzonenplan)
The GZP is a public national hazard zoning dataset operated by the **Forsttechnischer Dienst fuer Wildbach- und Lawinenverbauung (Federal Ministry BMLUK)** **(data.gv.at, n.d.-b)**. It provides a statutory hazard assessment under the Forstgesetz covering torrent, avalanche, landslide and erosion processes at parcel/address-level granularity across Austria **(INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-a)**. The source is accessed through an OGC map service and direct download in shapefile/GeoPackage format, and is free of charge as a High Value Dataset under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/138 **(INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-a)**. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because it is the strongest national source for mass-movement and torrent hazards, complementing rather than duplicating the BWV flood data, although hazard zones are mapped only within settled areas of relevance, so an unmatched portfolio location may indicate either no hazard or simply no mapping. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is ready on the same basis as the BWV flood layers: geocoding plus a WMS/WFS query or local spatial join yields a differentiating, parcel-level value per exposure within mapped municipalities.

### Hochwasserrisikozonierung Austria (hora)
HORA (Natural Hazard Overview and Risk Assessment Austria) is a publicly accessible Austrian WebGIS application, operated by the **Land-, Forst- und Wasserwirtschaftliche Rechenzentrum (LFRZ)** on behalf of the responsible federal ministry **(LFRZ, n.d.)**. It provides address- and cadastral-level initial information on natural hazards, currently covering flood, earthquake, storm, hail and snow **(HORA, n.d.)**, with further hazards such as landslide added over time through cooperation with GeoSphere Austria **(BMLUK, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through an interactive web interface via individual location queries, and is free to use. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because the underlying zoning is genuinely parcel-level and well-suited to individual validation, although bulk/API access is restricted under the terms of use, limiting it to spot-checking rather than systematic portfolio classification. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is not ready: this is a technical, not a granularity, limitation - the source would be portfolio-ready if bulk access were available.

### Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas (wri_aqueduct)
The Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas is a public water-risk dataset operated by the **World Resources Institute (WRI)** **(WRI, n.d.)**. It provides 13 modelled indicators covering water stress, drought risk and both riverine and coastal flood risk at sub-catchment (watershed) granularity globally **(WRI, n.d.-b)**. The source is accessed through a web map portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it fills a gap in the domestic data landscape for chronic water-related hazards, although its global watershed-level granularity is coarser than the parcel-level Austrian sources for the acute hazards it also covers. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is only partly ready: once geocoded and spatially joined to its watershed polygons, it differentiates between regions but not within a single watershed.

### Moody's Physical Risk Data (moodys_physical_risk)
This is a commercial dataset operated by **Moody's ESG Solutions** **(Moody's, n.d.)**. The underlying methodology originates from Four Twenty Seven, Inc., a physical-climate-risk data firm founded in 2012, in which Moody's Corporation acquired a majority stake in July 2019 as a Moody's Investors Service affiliate **(Moody's, 2019)**; the acquisition was reported in Moody's own 2019 Form 10-Q as operating within the MIS reportable segment **(Moody's, 2019b)**. It provides proprietary modelled physical climate risk scores covering six hazards (floods, heat stress, hurricanes and typhoons, sea-level rise, water stress, wildfires) at facility/object-level granularity globally **(Moody's, n.d.-b)**; note that the storm hazard is modelled as tropical cyclones specifically, which has limited relevance to Austria. The source is accessed through a commercial analytics platform and an API, and is paid, subject to a commercial licence agreement. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data points 0050 and 0090–0130 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it offers ready-made, high-granularity risk scores integrable via API with low processing effort, although its cost structure is typically designed for larger institutions rather than an SNCI's scale. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is fully ready: the API returns a differentiating, facility-level score directly, with no further spatial processing required.

### NATHAN Risk Suite (munich_re_nathan)
NATHAN Risk Suite is a commercial dataset operated by **Munich Re** **(Munich Re, n.d.)**. It provides proprietary modelled natural hazard and climate scenario scores covering multiple hazards at facility/object-level granularity globally, including scenarios extending to 2100 **(Munich Re, n.d.)**. Two access models exist: a subscription-based portfolio platform (Business/Corporate tiers, with API access, quote-based pricing) and a subscription-free, pay-per-location "On-Demand" service priced from EUR 100-160 per location depending on package size (Munich Re, n.d.-c). In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data points 0050 and 0090–0130 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because of its very extensive scenario depth and ready-made scores; unlike the quote-based subscription tiers geared towards larger institutions and reinsurers, the pay-per-location On-Demand option makes single-location or small-scale spot-checks accessible without a full portfolio subscription, though it is not designed for bulk portfolio processing. In portfolio-readiness terms, the subscription/API tier is fully ready: it returns a differentiating, facility-level score directly, with no further spatial processing required.

### World Bank Data Catalog / Risk Data Library (world_bank_gfdrr)
This is a public dataset collection operated by the **World Bank Group** in cooperation with **GFDRR** **(World Bank/GFDRR, n.d.)**, comprising both the historical-event Risk Data Library and the forward-looking, probabilistic ThinkHazard! hazard tool **(GFDRR, n.d.)**. It provides climate and disaster risk data covering multiple hazards, with ThinkHazard! resolving to district (ADM2) level via a public API and the Risk Data Library varying by dataset **(GFDRR, n.d.-b)**, and a development-country focus overall **(World Bank/GFDRR, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through a generic web portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant only as a context/validation reference, not to a specific data point. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable as a supplementary plausibility check because its district-level granularity is coarser than the parcel-level Austrian sources for acute hazards, though it still differentiates within Austria. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is only partly ready: once queried via the ThinkHazard! API, it differentiates between Austrian districts but not within a single district.

### Kryosphaeren-Monitoring Oesterreich (kryomon_at)
KryoMon.AT is a publicly funded research consortium coordinated by the **University of Graz**, involving multiple Austrian universities, the Austrian and Bavarian Academies of Sciences, GeoSphere Austria and several associations **(KryoMon.AT, n.d.; ORF Steiermark, 2026)**. It provides periodic reports on permafrost, glacier and snow trends covering permafrost hazard at station-based granularity across selected Austrian alpine sites **(KryoMon.AT, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through a reports/publications website and PDF downloads, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable as the best available current context on Austrian permafrost trends, although its station-based reporting cannot be overlaid with individual portfolio locations and is therefore unsuitable for exposure-level classification. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is not ready: the information exists only in narrative PDF reports, not as structured per-location data, so no automated pipeline can be built.

### SPARTACUS-v3 (geosphere_spartacus)
SPARTACUS-v3 is a public gridded climate dataset operated by **GeoSphere Austria**, the Austrian federal institute for geology, geophysics, climatology and meteorology **(GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-a)**. It provides observed daily, monthly and quarterly temperature, precipitation and sunshine-duration fields at 1km resolution across Austria since 1961, updated daily **(GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-a)**. The source is accessed through a web interface, a coordinate-based REST API and HTTP bulk download, and is free of charge, published under the CC0 1.0 Public Domain Dedication used for most GeoSphere Austria Data Hub datasets **(GeoSphere Austria/re3data, n.d.)**. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because it provides Austria's highest-resolution national gridded observation dataset, finer than Copernicus CDS' ~0.1-0.25 degree grid, with a coordinate-based API enabling direct point queries. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is ready: once geocoded, an API query per coordinate returns a differentiating, 1km-resolution value, though converting the raw climate variables into a specific heat-wave or heavy-precipitation indicator requires the bank's own threshold definition.

### WINFORE (geosphere_winfore_spei)
WINFORE is a public gridded drought-index dataset operated by **GeoSphere Austria** **(GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-b)**. It provides the Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) at 30-, 90- and 365-day aggregation windows, plus potential evapotranspiration, derived from SPARTACUS temperature and precipitation fields at 1km resolution across Austria, updated daily **(GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-b)**. The source is accessed through a web interface, a coordinate-based REST API and HTTP bulk download, and is free of charge, published under the CC0 1.0 Public Domain Dedication used for most GeoSphere Austria Data Hub datasets **(GeoSphere Austria/re3data, n.d.)**. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because it is a dedicated, purpose-built national drought index at fine resolution, filling a gap not covered by any other Austrian source in this matrix, although the SPEI value is a standardised statistical anomaly rather than an absolute water-deficit measure. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is ready: once geocoded, an API query per coordinate returns a differentiating, 1km-resolution SPEI value directly.

### Spectra (climate_x)
Spectra is a commercial physical climate risk platform operated by **Climate X Ltd**, a UK-based company founded in 2020 **(Technology Tools, n.d.)**. It provides asset-level modelled physical hazard scores and financial loss projections across up to 16 hazards (including flooding, storms, tropical cyclones, storm surge, heat stress, wildfires, landslides and subsidence), with scenario projections to 2100 **(Repath, n.d.; Net Zero Compare, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through a commercial SaaS platform and an API, and is paid, subject to a quote-based commercial licence; third-party estimates (not operator-confirmed) suggest a "mid-market" price range of roughly EUR 20,000-60,000 per year for a 30-100 asset portfolio **(Repath, n.d.)**. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data points 0050 and 0090–0130 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it offers ready-made, high-granularity asset-level scores integrable via API, although its cost and enterprise-SaaS positioning may still exceed a typical SNCI's budget scale. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is fully ready: the API returns a differentiating, asset-level score directly, with no further spatial processing required.

### ClimateScore Global (jupiter_intelligence)
ClimateScore Global is a commercial physical climate risk platform operated by **Jupiter Intelligence, Inc.**, a US company founded in 2017 **(aigreentools.com, n.d.)**. It provides asset-level modelled physical hazard scores and finance-grade loss metrics across 9 physical perils (including flood, heat, drought, tropical cyclones, wind and wildfire), across 3 emissions scenarios in 5-year increments to 2100, based on peer-reviewed CMIP6/ERA5 science **(aigreentools.com, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through a commercial SaaS platform and an API, and is paid, subject to a quote-based enterprise licence; Jupiter's own CEO has stated subscription pricing ranges from USD 25,000 to multi-million-dollar contracts depending on scope, with full portfolio deployments typically at six-figure annual commitments **(Trellis/Clancy, 2022; Repath, n.d.)**. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data points 0050 and 0090–0130 as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because of its extensive, scientifically validated hazard and financial-metric coverage; the lower end of its stated pricing range may be within reach for a well-resourced SNCI for a limited scope, although full portfolio deployments are likely well above a typical SNCI's budget scale. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is fully ready: the API returns a differentiating, asset-level score directly, with no further spatial processing required.

## Transition risk sources

### Firmenbuchnummer–OeNACE assignment / HVD (hvd_fbnr_oenace)
This is a public register operated by **Statistik Austria** **(Statistik Austria, n.d.)**. It provides the official OeNACE 2025 (NACE Rev. 2.1) economic-activity code for each Austrian company, keyed to the Firmenbuch number, covering the full national company register at entity-level granularity **(Statistik Austria, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through direct bulk download in CSV format and is free of charge under a CC BY 4.0 licence, published as a High Value Dataset under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/138. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to the NACE subsector dimension as a primary source. For an SNCI, the source is suitable because it is the only identified source that is simultaneously official, freely accessible in bulk and directly joinable to a loan portfolio via the Firmenbuch number, although companies without an assigned OeNACE code, and unregistered sole traders, remain unmatched. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is fully ready: a direct join on the Firmenbuchnummer assigns the OeNACE code to each exposure with no geocoding or spatial processing needed.

### Wirtschafts-Compass (wirtschafts_compass)
Wirtschafts-Compass is a commercial Austrian company-data service operated by **Compass-Verlag GmbH** **(Compass-Verlag, n.d.)**. It provides an editorially enriched OeNACE classification per entity, covering the Austrian company landscape at entity-level granularity, delivered with daily currency **(Compass-Verlag, n.d.)**. The source is accessed through an API and a web portal, and is paid, though the operator states that the finance and insurance sector is among its long-standing customers, meaning many Austrian banking-sector institutions may already hold an institutional licence **(Compass-Verlag, n.d.-b)**. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to the NACE subsector dimension as a secondary source. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable because it complements the HVD dataset operationally where an existing licence makes a single query simpler than a bulk join, although its classification is editorially enriched rather than the official statistical assignment. In portfolio-readiness terms, the source is fully ready on the same basis as HVD: the API returns a differentiating classification directly for each queried entity.

*(Other commercial providers offering Firmenbuch-based company-data queries may also be relevant but were not separately assessed in this matrix.)*
