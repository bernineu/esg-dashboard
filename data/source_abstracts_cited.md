# ESG Data Source Matrix - Source Abstracts (Artefact 2)

This document provides the full-text description of each of the 21 identified data sources, following the standardised documentation template (Abstract, Limitations, Suitability for SNCIs, Pricing, Licensing), presented as one continuous paragraph per source with citations. The Portfolio-ready pipeline field is intentionally not included here; it is a technical implementation note kept only in the matrix/dashboard (source_details.csv, ESG_Data_Source_Matrix_Structured.xlsx), not in this thesis-facing document. The matrix/dashboard also stores the same five fields separately per source for filtering purposes; the Abstract field there is a descriptive-only excerpt of the first sentences below, without the Limitations/Suitability/Pricing/Licensing reasoning, to avoid duplicating information already shown elsewhere in the dashboard.

## Physical risk sources

### Climate-ADAPT (eea_climate_adapt)
Climate-ADAPT is a public knowledge portal operated by the European Environment Agency (EEA) (EEA, n.d.-a). It provides pre-computed, peer-reviewed climate hazard indices via the European Climate Data Explorer (ECDE), covering heat, precipitation, flood, drought, storm and wildfire hazards at EU-wide scope and grid-based granularity (0.1-0.25 degrees, 5km for hydrology-based indices) (EEA/C3S, n.d.). The source is accessed through a web portal, a queryable API and direct download, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. The main limitation is incomplete hazard coverage. In particular, permafrost, landslide and soil erosion have no corresponding index at all, and heat stress and water stress are only covered via indirect proxies (UTCI; aridity), not dedicated indicators (EEA, 2021). Consequently, the source cannot serve as a comprehensive physical-risk data source on its own, unless complemented by other Austrian or EU sources for the missing hazards. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is providing ready-made, peer-reviewed hazard indices with documented methodology (EEA/C3S, n.d.), saving the index-definition effort that raw climate variables would require. It is particularly useful as a low-effort screening layer for the hazards it does cover. However, its suitability is reduced by incomplete hazard coverage and grid resolution coarser than Austria's parcel-level sources. It is therefore best used as a supplementary source alongside the dedicated Austrian hazard layers. The source is free of charge and available without registration (EEA, n.d.-a). No licence-specific restrictions on commercial or systematic use were identified beyond standard EU open-data attribution practice. The source is available under the same CC-BY 4.0 licence that applies to the underlying Copernicus Climate Data Store since 2 July 2025 (ECMWF Forum, 2025). Attribution is required when the data is reused. The licence permits commercial use and systematic portfolio processing.

### Risk Data Hub (RDH) (jrc_risk_data_hub)
The Risk Data Hub (RDH) is a public multi-hazard risk and loss data platform operated by the Joint Research Centre (JRC) in cooperation with the EU Disaster Risk Management Knowledge Centre (DRMKC) (JRC DRMKC, n.d.). It provides modelled hazard, exposure and historical-loss data covering flood, landslide, earthquake, wildfire and subsidence at NUTS 2/3 granularity across EU, EFTA and IPA countries (Antofie et al., 2023). The source is accessed through a web portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that the Vulnerability and Risk Analysis modules are documented by JRC itself as 'ongoing work'. In particular, only the Exposure Analysis and Historical Events modules provide reliably usable, hazard-verified layers, and several matrix hazards (heat stress, water stress, soil erosion, glacial lake, permafrost) are not covered at all. Consequently, the source cannot be treated as a complete multi-hazard reference, unless cross-checked against the specific hazard coverage documented per module. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is EU-wide consistent methodology combined with genuine hazard-and-exposure modelling for five hazards (Antofie et al., 2023). It is particularly useful for substantive risk determination at data point 0050. However, its suitability is reduced by NUTS 2/3-level granularity, coarser than Austria's parcel-level sources, and by being over-specified for the pure Geography reporting requirement. It is therefore best used as a primary source for hazard determination, complemented by finer Austrian layers where available. The source is free of charge and available without registration (JRC DRMKC, n.d.). No commercial-use restrictions were identified beyond standard attribution requirements. The source is available under an open EU data licence with attribution required, consistent with JRC's general data-sharing terms (JRC DRMKC, n.d.). The licence permits commercial use and systematic portfolio processing, provided that the source is appropriately credited.

### European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS) (effis_wildfire)
EFFIS (European Forest Fire Information System) is a public wildfire information service operated jointly by the JRC and the Copernicus Emergency Management Service (JRC/Copernicus EMS, n.d.). It provides a daily fire danger forecast and a historical burnt-area database covering wildfire hazard across Europe, the Middle East and North Africa at grid resolutions ranging from approximately 8-16km (forecast) to 250m/20m (observed burnt area) (EFFIS, n.d.-b). The source is accessed through an OGC map service (WMS/WFS) and direct download, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. The main limitation is that the source covers wildfire only, by design. In particular, the fire danger forecast grid (~8-16km) is coarse relative to Austria's size, covering the whole country with only a handful of grid cells, while the finer 250m/20m burnt-area layer is retrospective/observational rather than a forward-looking exposure indicator. Consequently, the source cannot support fine-grained forward-looking wildfire exposure classification on its own, unless combined with the observed burnt-area history for plausibility checks. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is being the only dedicated European wildfire dataset covering both forecast fire danger and observed fire history (JRC/Copernicus EMS, n.d.). It is particularly useful for national-level wildfire screening and historical plausibility checks. However, its suitability is reduced by the coarse forecast-grid resolution for a country the size of Austria and by the raw, unprocessed nature of both layers. It is therefore best used as a supplementary source for wildfire, complemented by own aggregation of the raw data. The source is free of charge and available under a CC BY 4.0 licence (EFFIS, n.d.-c). Attribution is required when the data is reused. The source is available under a CC BY 4.0 licence, as confirmed on EFFIS's own dedicated data-licence page (EFFIS, n.d.-c). Attribution is required when the data is reused. The licence permits commercial use and systematic portfolio processing.

### Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) (copernicus_cds)
The Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) is a public climate data repository operated by ECMWF on behalf of the EU (ECMWF, n.d.), comprising over 140 datasets of raw and derived climate variables and reanalysis/projection data. It provides raw climate variables (e.g. temperature, precipitation, wind fields) covering multiple physical hazards at grid resolutions of roughly 0.1-0.25 degrees globally, with an EU focus. The source is accessed through a web portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that the source provides raw climate variables rather than ready-made hazard indicators. In particular, every use requires selecting the correct raw variable(s) and building a bespoke climatological/GIS pipeline, and individual sub-datasets may carry stricter licence terms than the general CC-BY 4.0 (e.g. E-OBS, restricted to non-commercial research/education use). Consequently, the source cannot be used directly for exposure classification, unless the institution builds and validates its own index-derivation methodology. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is being authoritative, EU-wide consistent and covering the broadest range of underlying climate variables of any source in the matrix (ECMWF, n.d.). It is particularly useful as the raw-data foundation when no ready-made indicator exists elsewhere. However, its suitability is reduced by the substantial climatological and GIS processing effort required before any variable becomes an exposure classification. It is therefore best used as a fallback source for hazards not covered by ready-made products, rather than as an operational primary source. The source is free of charge (ECMWF, n.d.), though registration is required to download data. The source is available under CC-BY 4.0 since 2 July 2025, replacing the previous bespoke "Licence to Use Copernicus Products" (Version 1.2, Nov 2019) that governed CDS access before that date (ECMWF Forum, 2025). Attribution is required when the data is reused. Individual sub-datasets may carry stricter third-party terms and should be verified per dataset before use.

### EBA ESG Dashboard (eba_esg_dashboard)
The EBA ESG Dashboard is a public statistics dashboard operated by the European Banking Authority (EBA) (EBA, n.d.-a). It provides aggregated ESG risk indicators at EU bank level, covering both physical and transition climate risk at institution/portfolio-level granularity across the EU/EEA (EBA, n.d.-a). The source is accessed through a statistics dashboard, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to benchmarking as a context-only source. The main limitation is that the source provides only aggregate EU bank-level statistics (EBA, n.d.-a). In particular, no download or API access exists and no per-exposure data is available whatsoever. Consequently, the source cannot be used to populate any individual exposure in D 01.01, unless it is used purely for peer-comparison or benchmarking purposes. For an SNCI, the source is not suitable for populating individual exposures. Its main advantage is providing an authoritative EU/EEA peer-comparison benchmark (EBA, n.d.-a). It is particularly useful for contextualising an institution's own physical and transition risk exposure against sector averages. However, its suitability is reduced by the complete absence of per-exposure or downloadable data. It is therefore best used purely as a context/benchmarking source, not as an operational data source. The source is free of charge and available without registration (EBA, n.d.-a). The source is available under the EBA's standard open-data terms, free of charge and without registration (EBA, n.d.-a). No restrictions on commercial or systematic use beyond standard attribution practice were identified.

### ECB Climate-Related Physical Risk Indicators (ecb_physical_risk_indicators)
This is a public methodological and statistical resource operated by the European Central Bank (ECB) (ECB, n.d.). It provides a documented physical-risk indicator methodology and euro-area aggregate statistics, covering coastal flooding, river flooding, wildfires, landslides, subsidence, windstorms, water stress, droughts and rainfall variation at institution/portfolio-level granularity (ECB, n.d.). The source is accessed through a statistics dashboard, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a methodological source. The main limitation is that the underlying supervisory data (AnaCredit/RIAD) is not accessible to an SNCI. In particular, the published output is aggregate/dashboard statistics with no per-exposure data. Consequently, the source cannot be used directly to classify individual exposures, unless the bank replicates the documented methodology using its own AnaCredit-equivalent data plus JRC/Copernicus hazard layers. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is a transparent, replicable methodology covering nine physical hazards with an authoritative EU-level pedigree (ECB, n.d.). It is particularly useful as a methodological template for building an in-house classification approach. However, its suitability is reduced by the inaccessibility of the ECB's own underlying supervisory data to a non-significant institution. It is therefore best used as a methodological anchor rather than as a direct operational data source. The source is free of charge and available without registration (ECB, n.d.). The source is available under the ECB's standard open-data terms, free of charge and without registration (ECB, n.d.). No restrictions on commercial or systematic use beyond standard attribution practice were identified.

### Hochwasserueberflutungsflaechen HQ30/HQ100/HQ300 (hq_flood_extent)
This is a national flood extent dataset, published via the INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, with the Bundesministerium fuer Land- und Forstwirtschaft, Klima- und Umweltschutz, Regionen und Wasserwirtschaft (BMLUK) as data owner and Umweltbundesamt GmbH as technical operator (INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-b). It provides harmonised flood extent layers for the 30-, 100- and 300-year return periods, covering flood hazard at parcel/address-level granularity across Austria. The source is accessed through OGC WMS/WFS services and direct GML/shapefile download, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is data currency. In particular, the underlying data has a Datenstand of October 2020, roughly six years old as of this review, and is a composite of several underlying sources (ABU, HORA, BWV/WLV Gefahrenzonenplaene) with potentially inconsistent vintages. Consequently, the source cannot be assumed to reflect flood-protection works or land-use changes made since 2020, unless cross-checked against more recent local information. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is combining several underlying Austrian flood sources into a single, harmonised layer with a standard OGC interface and no access restriction, unlike HORA (INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-b). It is particularly useful as the primary operational flood layer for populating data point 0050. However, its suitability is reduced somewhat by the dataset's data currency. It is therefore best used as the primary flood-hazard source, complemented by periodic currency checks. The source is free of charge and available under a CC BY 4.0 licence (INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-b). The source is available under a CC BY 4.0 licence (INSPIRE Geoportal Oesterreich, n.d.-b). Attribution is required when the data is reused. The licence permits commercial use and systematic portfolio processing.

### WLV Gefahrenzonenplan (GZP) (wlv_gefahrenzonenplan)
The Gefahrenzonenplan (GZP) is a public national hazard zoning dataset operated by the Forsttechnischer Dienst fuer Wildbach- und Lawinenverbauung (Federal Ministry BMLUK) (data.gv.at, n.d.-b). It provides a statutory hazard assessment for torrents, avalanches and mass-movement processes, covering these hazards at parcel/address-level granularity across Austria. The source is accessed through an OGC map service (WMS/WFS) and direct download, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that hazards are mapped only within areas of spatial relevance, meaning settled parts of a municipality with designated torrent or avalanche catchments (data.gv.at, n.d.-b). In particular, some zone plans remain available only in analogue form, and a portfolio location without a mapped result is ambiguous - it may indicate no hazard or simply no mapping. Consequently, the source cannot be used to conclusively rule out hazard for unmapped locations, unless cross-checked against the underlying zone-plan status. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is being the strongest national source for mass-movement and torrent hazards, based on a statutory hazard assessment under the Forstgesetz (data.gv.at, n.d.-b). It is particularly useful for classifying torrent, avalanche and related mass-movement exposure at data point 0050. However, its suitability is reduced by incomplete settlement-area-only mapping and residual analogue-only zone plans. It is therefore best used as the primary source for these hazards, with unmapped locations flagged for manual review. The source is free of charge and available under the High Value Dataset regime (data.gv.at, n.d.-b). The source is available as a High Value Dataset under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/138, free of charge with bulk download permitted via the WLV download service in ESRI shapefile format. The licence permits commercial use and systematic portfolio processing.

### Hochwasserrisikozonierung Austria (HORA) (hora)
HORA (Natural Hazard Overview and Risk Assessment Austria) is a publicly accessible Austrian WebGIS application, operated by the Land-, Forst- und Wasserwirtschaftliche Rechenzentrum (LFRZ) on behalf of the responsible federal ministry (LFRZ, n.d.). It provides address- and cadastral-level initial information on natural hazards, currently covering flood, earthquake, storm, hail and snow, at parcel/address-level granularity across Austria (HORA, n.d.). The source is accessed through an interactive web interface via individual location queries, and is free to use. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is the restricted use of the service for systematic portfolio-level analysis (LFRZ, n.d.). In particular, the source is designed primarily for individual location queries and does not provide unrestricted bulk download or a documented portfolio-level API, and the displayed information is intended as initial risk information rather than a legally binding assessment. Consequently, HORA cannot be used as the sole source for automated classification of an entire loan portfolio, unless a separate access agreement is obtained from the operator. For an SNCI, HORA is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is Austrian address-level coverage combined with simple web-based access (HORA, n.d.). It is particularly useful for individual exposure checks and plausibility assessments. However, its suitability is reduced by the restricted portfolio-level access and the non-binding nature of the displayed information. It is therefore best used as a supplementary validation source rather than as the sole operational source for populating data point 0050. The source is free of charge for individual queries. A separate agreement with the operator (LFRZ) would be required for portfolio-level or bulk use. The source is subject to specific terms of use that restrict bulk download and automated access (LFRZ, n.d.); the service is intended for individual location queries only. A separate agreement with the operator would be required for systematic portfolio-level use or regulatory reporting purposes.

### Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas (wri_aqueduct)
The Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas is a public water-risk dataset operated by the World Resources Institute (WRI) (WRI, n.d.). It provides 13 modelled indicators covering water stress, drought risk and riverine/coastal flood risk at sub-catchment (watershed) granularity globally (WRI, n.d.-b). The source is accessed through a web map portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. The main limitation is coarse granularity relative to Austria's parcel-level sources. In particular, the source resolves only to sub-catchment (watershed) level, so exposures within the same watershed share an identical value, and it is a global tool not locally calibrated or validated for Austria specifically. Consequently, the source cannot differentiate exposures within a geographically concentrated Austrian portfolio, unless combined with finer national sources for the acute hazards it also covers. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is providing dedicated, named indicators for chronic water-related hazards (water stress, drought) otherwise underrepresented among Austrian sources. It is particularly useful as a supplementary chronic-risk layer. However, its suitability is reduced by watershed-level granularity, coarser than the parcel-level Austrian sources for acute hazards. It is therefore best used as a supplementary source for water stress and drought, not as a primary classification layer. The source is free of charge and available without registration (WRI, n.d.). The source is described by the operator as free and openly accessible for any application, with attribution requested (WRI, n.d.). No formally stated open licence name was identified on the operator's own materials, and no commercial-use restriction was found.

### Moody's Physical Risk Data (moodys_physical_risk)
This is a commercial dataset operated by Moody's ESG Solutions (Moody's, n.d.). It provides proprietary modelled physical climate risk scores covering floods, heat stress, hurricanes and typhoons, sea-level rise, water stress and wildfires at facility/object-level granularity globally (Moody's, n.d.-b). The source is accessed through a commercial analytics platform and an API, and is paid. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that confirmed Austria-relevant coverage is narrower than the product's general marketing suggests (Moody's, n.d.-b). In particular, storm is modelled as tropical cyclones specifically, which does not affect Austria, and a distinct heat-wave product plus drought coverage could not be confirmed at all. Consequently, the source cannot be relied upon for storm, heat-wave or drought classification of Austrian exposures, unless the operator confirms specific coverage directly. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is ready-made, high-granularity facility-level risk scores integrable via API with low processing effort (Moody's, n.d.). It is particularly useful for flood, heat stress, water stress and wildfire classification. However, its suitability is reduced by a commercial cost structure typically designed for larger institutions and by narrower-than-marketed Austria-relevant hazard coverage. It is therefore best used as a primary source only for the confirmed hazards, subject to cost negotiation appropriate to SNCI scale. Pricing is not publicly disclosed. Access is provided through a commercial analytics platform and API under a quote-based licence agreement (Moody's, n.d.). No product-specific price indication was identified; publicly available estimates for other Moody's products should therefore not be used as a proxy. The price information should be treated as not independently verified. The source is subject to a commercial licence agreement (Moody's, n.d.). Access terms, API scope and portfolio size limits depend on the specific subscription negotiated with Moody's. The licence terms should be reviewed specifically for D 01.01 reporting use cases.

### NATHAN Risk Suite (munich_re_nathan)
NATHAN Risk Suite is a commercial dataset operated by Munich Re (Munich Re, n.d.). It provides proprietary modelled natural hazard and climate scenario scores covering 15 hazards, including river flood, extratropical storm, wildfire and landslide, at facility/object-level granularity globally, with scenarios extending to 2100 (Munich Re, n.d.-b). The source is accessed through a commercial analytics platform and an API, and is paid. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that heat, drought and water stress coverage could not be confirmed by name (Munich Re, n.d.-b). In particular, these hazards are only advertised via a separate, unnamed 'Climate Change Edition' covering '13 climate hazards' without individual naming in the materials reviewed. Consequently, the source cannot be relied upon for heat, drought or water stress classification with the same confidence as its 15 named Natural Hazards Edition hazards, unless the operator confirms specific coverage directly. For an SNCI, the source is suitable for its confirmed hazards. Its main advantage is very extensive scenario depth (to 2100) and ready-made facility-level scores for 15 explicitly named hazards, including landslide and extratropical storm. It is particularly useful for portfolio-scale exposure scoring via API. However, its suitability is reduced by pricing typically geared towards larger institutions and reinsurers, and by unconfirmed heat/drought/water-stress coverage. It is therefore best used as a primary source for the confirmed hazards, or via the accessible pay-per-location On-Demand option for smaller-scale use. Pricing follows two distinct access models (Munich Re, n.d.-c). The On-Demand service uses pay-per-location pricing, with publicly displayed prices ranging from approximately EUR 100 to EUR 160 per location depending on package size. Portfolio-scale access through the Natural Hazards Edition or Climate Change Edition is subscription-based and quote-based, with API access available in the relevant business tiers. The publicly displayed On-Demand prices are not directly comparable with portfolio subscription costs. The source is subject to a commercial licence agreement. The On-Demand service requires no subscription and is billed per location; portfolio-scale subscription tiers (Natural Hazards Edition, Climate Change Edition) determine API access and portfolio size limits. The licence terms should be reviewed specifically for D 01.01 reporting use cases.

### World Bank Data Catalog / Risk Data Library (RDL) (world_bank_gfdrr)
This is a public dataset collection operated by the World Bank Group in cooperation with GFDRR (World Bank/GFDRR, n.d.), comprising the Risk Data Library (downloadable hazard datasets) and the ThinkHazard! screening tool. It provides flood, landslide, wildfire, extreme-heat and water-scarcity hazard information at district (ADM2) to dataset-dependent granularity, with a development-country focus (GFDRR, n.d.). The source is accessed through a web portal, direct download and an API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a secondary source. The main limitation is granularity that is not uniform across the source. In particular, ThinkHazard! resolves only to district (ADM2) level, while Risk Data Library datasets vary individually in resolution, and storm coverage via ThinkHazard!'s 'cyclone' hazard model is tropical-cyclone-oriented with unconfirmed applicability to Austria. Consequently, the source cannot differentiate exposures within a single Austrian district, unless combined with finer national sources. For an SNCI, the source is conditionally suitable. Its main advantage is confirmed, commonly-available probabilistic coverage for flood, landslide, extreme heat, water scarcity and wildfire, accessible free via API (GFDRR, n.d.). It is particularly useful for plausibility checks and district-level screening. However, its suitability is reduced by district-level, not parcel-level, granularity. It is therefore best used as a supplementary or validation source rather than as the primary operational layer. The source is free of charge and available without registration (World Bank/GFDRR, n.d.). The source is available under the World Bank's open-data terms, free of charge and without registration (World Bank/GFDRR, n.d.). No restrictions on commercial or systematic use beyond standard attribution practice were identified.

### Kryosphaeren-Monitoring Oesterreich (KryoMon.AT) (kryomon_at)
KryoMon.AT is a publicly funded research consortium coordinated by the University of Graz, involving multiple Austrian universities, the Austrian and Bavarian Academies of Sciences, GeoSphere Austria and several associations (KryoMon.AT, n.d.; ORF Steiermark, 2026). It provides periodic reports on permafrost, glacier and snow trends covering permafrost hazard at station-based granularity across selected Austrian alpine sites (KryoMon.AT, n.d.). The source is accessed through a reports/publications website with PDF downloads, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a context-only source. The main limitation is that the information exists only as narrative PDF reports, not as structured per-location data (KryoMon.AT, n.d.). In particular, coverage is station-/object-based rather than a nationwide spatial hazard layer. Consequently, the source cannot be overlaid with individual portfolio locations, unless the bank undertakes manual, qualitative interpretation per region. For an SNCI, the source is not suitable for systematic classification. Its main advantage is being the best available current context on Austrian permafrost trends, produced by an authoritative multi-institutional research consortium (KryoMon.AT, n.d.; ORF Steiermark, 2026). It is particularly useful for background discussion and methodological context in the reporting narrative. However, its suitability is reduced by the complete absence of structured, location-specific data. It is therefore best used purely as a context source, not as an operational data source. The source is free of charge and available without registration (KryoMon.AT, n.d.). The source is available under the reporting consortium's general open-access publication terms, free of charge, with attribution expected per standard academic practice (KryoMon.AT, n.d.). No commercial-use restriction was identified.

### SPARTACUS-v3 (GeoSphere Austria) (geosphere_spartacus)
SPARTACUS-v3 is a public gridded climate dataset operated by GeoSphere Austria, the Austrian federal institute for geology, geophysics, climatology and meteorology (GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-a). It provides observed daily, monthly and quarterly temperature, precipitation and sunshine-duration fields, covering heat and precipitation hazards at 1km grid granularity across Austria since 1961 (GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-a). The source is accessed through a web interface, a coordinate-based REST API and HTTP bulk download, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that the source provides raw climate variables, not a pre-classified hazard or risk score (GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-a). In particular, converting temperature or precipitation fields into a heat-wave or heavy-precipitation exposure indicator requires the institution's own threshold definition. Consequently, the source cannot be used directly for exposure classification, unless the bank first defines and validates its own index methodology. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is Austria's highest-resolution national gridded observation dataset, finer than Copernicus CDS, with a coordinate-based API enabling direct point queries (GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-a). It is particularly useful as the primary raw-data foundation for heat and heavy-precipitation indices tailored to Austrian conditions. However, its suitability is reduced by the own-methodology effort required to convert raw variables into a hazard indicator. It is therefore best used as a primary source for institutions willing to define their own threshold-based indices. The source is free of charge and available without registration (GeoSphere Austria/re3data, n.d.). The source is available under the Creative Commons Public Domain Dedication (CC0 1.0), per GeoSphere Austria's own repository documentation, free for any use including commercial. No attribution is legally required, though it is good practice. The licence permits systematic portfolio processing.

### WINFORE (GeoSphere Austria) (geosphere_winfore_spei)
WINFORE is a public gridded drought-index dataset operated by GeoSphere Austria (GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-b). It provides the Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) at 30-, 90- and 365-day aggregation, covering drought hazard at 1km grid granularity across Austria, derived from SPARTACUS temperature and precipitation fields. The source is accessed through a web interface, a coordinate-based REST API and HTTP bulk download, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is that SPEI is a standardised statistical anomaly index, not an absolute water-deficit value (GeoSphere Austria, n.d.-b). In particular, it is derived via a normal-distribution transform over two climate reference periods, so its interpretation requires understanding this transform rather than reading a direct precipitation deficit. Consequently, the source cannot be interpreted at face value without methodological context, unless the reporting institution documents the SPEI transform in its own methodology notes. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is being a dedicated, purpose-built national drought index at fine (1km) resolution, filling a gap not covered by any other Austrian source in this matrix. It is particularly useful as the primary drought-classification source for data point 0050. However, its suitability is reduced somewhat by the need to correctly interpret the standardised anomaly value. It is therefore best used as the primary drought source, with a brief methodology note on SPEI interpretation. The source is free of charge and available without registration (GeoSphere Austria/re3data, n.d.). The source is available under the Creative Commons Public Domain Dedication (CC0 1.0), per GeoSphere Austria's own repository documentation, free for any use including commercial. No attribution is legally required, though it is good practice.

### Spectra (Climate X) (climate_x)
Spectra is a commercial physical climate risk platform operated by Climate X Ltd, a UK-based company founded in 2020 (Technology Tools, n.d.). It provides asset-level modelled physical hazard scores and financial loss projections across up to 16 hazards, including flooding, storms, heat stress, wildfires and landslides, at asset/facility-level granularity globally, with scenario projections to 2100 (Repath, n.d.). The source is accessed through a commercial SaaS platform and an API, and is paid. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. ### Copernicus Ready-Made Climate Indicators (ERA5-HEAT, SMHI Hydrology) (copernicus_climate_indicators)
This is a set of standalone, ready-made physical hazard indicator products, published as individual datasets on the Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS), distinct from the general-purpose raw climate variables covered by the separate copernicus_cds entry (ECMWF, n.d.). It provides ERA5-HEAT (UTCI/heat stress) and the SMHI hydrology dataset (flood, water stress/aridity, soil-moisture drought), covering these four hazards at grid granularity across Europe (Di Napoli et al., 2021; SMHI/C3S, n.d.). The source is accessed through direct download and a queryable API, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to data point 0050 as a primary source. The main limitation is narrower hazard breadth than the full Copernicus CDS catalogue. In particular, only heat stress, flood, water stress and soil-moisture drought are covered as standalone products; meteorological (SPI-3) drought and the temperature/precipitation/wind-based ECDE indices (heat wave, heavy precipitation, storm) remain accessible only via raw copernicus_cds variables or the Climate-ADAPT-curated bundle, and wildfire is documented separately under effis_wildfire. Consequently, the source cannot serve as a comprehensive hazard reference on its own, unless complemented by copernicus_cds, Climate-ADAPT and effis_wildfire for the remaining hazards. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is that each product is a purpose-built, documented hazard indicator with no own index-definition effort required, unlike raw Copernicus CDS variables (Di Napoli et al., 2021; SMHI/C3S, n.d.). It is particularly useful for heat stress, flood, water stress and soil-moisture drought classification at low processing effort. However, its suitability is reduced by its narrower hazard breadth compared with the full CDS catalogue. It is therefore best used as a primary source for its four covered hazards, complemented by other sources for the remainder. The source is free of charge (ECMWF, n.d.), though registration is required to download data via the CDS. The source is available under CC-BY 4.0 since 2 July 2025, the same licence as copernicus_cds (ECMWF Forum, 2025), replacing the prior "Licence to Use Copernicus Products" (Version 1.2, Nov 2019). Attribution is required when the data is reused. The licence permits commercial use and systematic portfolio processing.

### Firmenbuchnummer OeNACE Zuordnung (HVD) (hvd_fbnr_oenace)
This is a public register operated by Statistik Austria (Statistik Austria, n.d.). It provides the official OeNACE 2025 (NACE Rev. 2.1) economic-activity code for each Austrian company, keyed to the Firmenbuch number, covering the full national company register at entity-level granularity. The source is accessed through direct bulk download in CSV format, and is free of charge. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to the NACE subsector dimension as a primary source. The main limitation is incomplete company coverage (Statistik Austria, n.d.). In particular, companies without an assigned OeNACE code remain unmatched, and unregistered sole traders are out of scope of the Firmenbuch-based register. Consequently, the source cannot classify every possible counterparty in an SNCI's loan portfolio, unless a complementary source is used for the unmatched residual. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is being simultaneously official, freely accessible in bulk and directly joinable to a loan portfolio via the Firmenbuchnummer (Statistik Austria, n.d.). It is particularly useful as the primary operational source for populating the NACE subsector dimension. However, its suitability is reduced slightly by unmatched companies and out-of-scope sole traders. It is therefore best used as the primary transition-risk classification source, complemented by Wirtschafts-Compass where an existing licence is operationally simpler. ### Wirtschafts-Compass (wirtschafts_compass)
Wirtschafts-Compass is a commercial Austrian company-data service operated by Compass-Verlag GmbH (Compass-Verlag, n.d.). It provides an editorially enriched OeNACE classification per entity, covering the Austrian company landscape at entity-level granularity, delivered with daily currency. The source is accessed through an API and a web portal, and is paid. In the context of Template D 01.01, it is relevant to the NACE subsector dimension as a primary source. The main limitation is that the classification is editorially enriched rather than the official statistical assignment (Compass-Verlag, n.d.). In particular, cost is institution-dependent, since some banks may already hold a sector licence while others would face a new subscription cost. Consequently, the source cannot be treated as an authoritative regulatory reference on the same footing as the official HVD dataset, unless used alongside HVD for cross-validation. For an SNCI, the source is suitable. Its main advantage is being directly usable, daily-updated and API-accessible, with existing institutional access reported in parts of the Austrian banking sector (Compass-Verlag, n.d.-b). It is particularly useful where an existing licence makes a single query operationally simpler than a bulk HVD join. However, its suitability is reduced by cost for institutions without an existing licence and by its non-official classification status. It is therefore best used as a primary operational source alongside the free HVD dataset. Pricing is publicly disclosed for standard access: pay-per-query, from EUR 6 (excl. VAT) per quarter for up to 10 queries via the WKO-member service, or from EUR 24 per year for individual/basic access (Compass-Verlag, n.d.-c; Compass-Verlag, n.d.-d). Institutional/enterprise API pricing is not publicly listed and should be confirmed directly with the provider. The source is subject to a commercial licence agreement (Compass-Verlag, n.d.-c). Standard access is pay-per-query with published pricing; institutional API access terms depend on the specific subscription.
